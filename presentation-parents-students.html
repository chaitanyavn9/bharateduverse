Our Children Are Those Eight Farmers

Every year in India, millions of students finish their 10th or 12th. And almost all of them look left and right — and do what everyone else is doing. Computer Science. Engineering. Medical. MBA.

"Not because they love it. Not because there's a job waiting. But because their neighbour's son did it. Because the coaching centre near their house teaches it."

The market gets flooded. Lakhs of engineers graduate every year and struggle to find jobs in their field. Meanwhile, there are hundreds of other paths — oceanographers, quantum researchers, food technologists, sustainability experts, aerospace engineers — where demand is high and supply is almost zero.

Nobody told them those paths existed. Nobody showed them where the opportunities actually are. That's the problem we're solving.

Real Situations, Real Decisions

Stories That Could Be Your Story

👧
Priya from Guntur — after 10th

Priya loves biology and the ocean. Everyone told her — take BiPC, become a doctor. But NEET is brutal, seats are few, fees are high. Nobody told her about Oceanography at Andhra University Vizag, or that INCOIS — India's ocean science HQ — is right there in Hyderabad, or that India's deep sea mission Samudrayaan just got ₹4,000 Cr in funding. She could have built a career no one in her village has ever imagined.

👦
Ravi's father from Kurnool — confused parent

Ravi scored 92% in 10th. His father wants him to do engineering. Ravi loves numbers and wants to start something of his own. Nobody told Ravi's father that Actuarial Science — which Ravi would love — has a starting salary of ₹6-12L, is one of the hardest professions globally, and the whole of India has barely a few hundred qualified actuaries. Low supply. Very high demand. That's the farmer who grows in summer.

👨‍👩‍👧
A family from Vizag — investing their savings

They sold land to pay for their daughter's private engineering seat. Four years later she graduated — like 15 lakh others that year. If someone had shown them the government college route through EAMCET, the scholarship options, or the Polytechnic → LEET → BTech path that costs a fraction — they'd still have that land, and their daughter would have the same degree.
